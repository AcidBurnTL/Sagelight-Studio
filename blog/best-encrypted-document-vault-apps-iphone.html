Best encrypted document vault apps for iPhone: an honest comparison

The best encrypted document vault for an iPhone is one where only you hold the encryption keys, that you'll actually use, and that works on every device you need. If you only use Apple devices and want something built around documents and expiry dates, a dedicated vault such as SageDocs (which we make) fits. If you need Android or Windows too, 1Password, Bitwarden or Proton Drive are stronger choices. Apple's own tools work well once you turn on Advanced Data Protection. Below is how each option handles the keys, the platforms and the price, as of 13 October 2026.

Our bias, up front: we make SageDocs, one of the apps in this list. We've tried to describe every other option using only what its maker states in its own documentation, linked in the Sources section, and to say plainly where our app is the weaker choice.

How we compared them

For identity documents, one question matters more than any feature list: who holds the key that decrypts the file? If the provider holds it, your documents are only as safe as the provider's systems, staff and account-recovery process. If only your devices hold it (end-to-end encryption), the provider stores data it can't read. We also looked at:

  • Platforms: iPhone only, Apple only, or every device you own.
  • Recovery: what happens if you forget your password. With true end-to-end encryption, the honest answer is often "nobody can help you".
  • Price as stated on the vendor's pricing page on 13 October 2026. Prices change and can differ by country.
  • Document handling: storage limits, sharing and whether the tool tracks expiry dates.

Apple Notes with locked notes

Locked notes are the easiest free option on an iPhone. You can lock a note with your device passcode or a separate Notes password, unlock it with Face ID or Touch ID, and Apple says both options use end-to-end encryption for iCloud accounts. If you forget a separate Notes password, Apple can't recover those notes.

The catch is what you can put in them. Apple's own list says you can't lock notes that contain PDFs, tags, audio, video, or Keynote, Pages or Numbers files, and you can't lock notes you've shared. A photo of a passport page works; a PDF of a signed contract doesn't. There's also no expiry-date field, so reminders live elsewhere.

Good for: a few ID photos, quickly, at no cost.

iCloud Drive and the Files app (with Advanced Data Protection)

Files you save to iCloud Drive are encrypted, but under Apple's default Standard Data Protection the keys for iCloud Drive are held by Apple. Turning on Advanced Data Protection moves iCloud Drive (and Photos, Notes and backups) to end-to-end encryption, with keys held only on your trusted devices.

It's opt-in for a reason. You need two-factor authentication, up-to-date software on every signed-in device, and a recovery contact or a 28-character recovery key, because Apple no longer has the keys to help you recover your data. Apple also states that it can no longer offer Advanced Data Protection to new users in the United Kingdom.

Good for: people who already keep everything in Files and are comfortable managing a recovery key.

1Password

1Password is a password manager, but it has a dedicated Document item type and lets you attach files to other items. Its security model is strong: data is end-to-end encrypted with AES-GCM-256, and your account password is combined with a 128-bit Secret Key that 1Password never receives. Individual accounts include 1 GB of file storage (1 GB per person on Families).

On 13 October 2026, the pricing page listed Individual at $2.99/month billed yearly and Families at $4.49/month billed yearly as a first-year promotion for new customers (regular prices $3.99 and $5.99), with a 14-day free trial. Apps cover iPhone, Android, Mac, Windows, Linux and browsers, and family members can share a vault.

Good for: anyone who also needs a password manager, or uses Android or Windows alongside an iPhone.

Bitwarden

Bitwarden's file attachments are a Premium feature. Bitwarden says attachments are encrypted and decrypted locally and never stored unencrypted on its servers. Each account gets up to 5 GB of attachments, with files up to 500 MB (100 MB when uploading from mobile). Premium was listed at $19.80 per year on 13 October 2026, and Bitwarden has apps for iOS, Android, Windows, macOS, Linux and the web.

Good for: the lowest price for end-to-end encrypted files across every platform, if you don't mind storing documents as attachments inside password-manager items.

Proton Drive

Proton Drive is a full cloud drive with end-to-end encryption: files are encrypted with your keys, and Proton says it can't access your files or see their names. The free plan includes 5 GB; Drive Plus was listed at 200 GB for $4.99/month on monthly billing on 13 October 2026. Apps cover iOS, Android, Windows and macOS, plus a web app.

As with every end-to-end encrypted service, recovery needs planning. Proton's support pages warn that if you reset your password by email or SMS without a recovery phrase or file, you may lose access to your existing data.

Good for: people who want a general private cloud drive, not just a document vault, across Apple and non-Apple devices.

SageDocs (our app)

SageDocs is built only for important documents: IDs, passports, contracts, insurance and medical papers. Every file is encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM, and keys are derived from your master PIN with PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 iterations) and kept in the Keychain behind Face ID, Touch ID or the PIN. Sync through your own iCloud is optional; Apple stores only ciphertext. There's no Sagelight account and no Sagelight server, so we never see your documents.

What it does that the general tools above aren't built around: expiry tracking. You record when a passport, ID card, insurance policy or vehicle registration expires, and SageDocs reminds you on your device beforehand.

Where it's weaker, honestly:

  • Apple only. iPhone (iOS 17+), iPad (iPadOS 17+) and Mac (macOS 14+). No Android, Windows or web app.
  • No shared family vault. You can decrypt and share a single document when you choose to, but there's no shared space like 1Password's family vaults.
  • No recovery if you forget your master PIN. That's the point of the design, but it means you need to choose a PIN you'll remember.

It costs $2.99/month or $24.99/year after a 7-day free trial.

Which one should you choose?

  • You only need a few ID photos and spend nothing: Apple Notes locked notes.
  • You live in the Files app: iCloud Drive with Advanced Data Protection turned on, plus a recovery key you keep safe.
  • You also use Android or Windows: 1Password, Bitwarden or Proton Drive.
  • You want a private vault built for documents and their expiry dates on Apple devices: SageDocs.

Whatever you choose, the basics matter more than the brand: a strong device passcode, two-factor authentication on the account, and no stray copies left in your photo library or email. Our guide on storing passport and ID scans safely covers that cleanup.

For expiry dates, the general-purpose tools above are designed as password managers or file stores, and the documentation we reviewed doesn't describe document-expiry reminders. If you pick one of them, add each expiry date to Calendar or Reminders yourself, as described in how to get reminded before your passport or ID expires.

FAQ

Is a password manager a good place to store passport scans?

Yes, if it's end-to-end encrypted. 1Password and Bitwarden both encrypt files on your device before upload. The trade-offs are storage limits (1 GB per person on 1Password, 5 GB on Bitwarden Premium) and that documents become attachments inside a password tool.

Are Apple Notes locked notes end-to-end encrypted?

Apple says locked notes use end-to-end encryption for iCloud accounts, whether you lock them with your device passcode or a separate Notes password. You can't lock notes containing PDFs, tags, audio, video or iWork files.

Is iCloud Drive encrypted end to end?

Only with Advanced Data Protection turned on. Under the default Standard Data Protection, Apple holds the keys for iCloud Drive.

What happens if I forget the password to an end-to-end encrypted vault?

Usually, nobody can decrypt your files for you. Apple, Proton and SageDocs all state this in some form. Set up the recovery options the service offers, or keep a recovery key somewhere safe offline.
