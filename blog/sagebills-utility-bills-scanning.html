How to scan and archive utility bills on iPhone, without an account

In short: a bill is easy to find a year later if you record it today, correctly and in the same way as the others. In SageBills you photograph the bill, and the app reads the amount, the date and the supplier directly on the phone, with Apple's text recognition (Vision). You check what it read, choose the category and save. You do not need an account, and the data does not go to us. Below you will find how to take a good photo, what to check after scanning and what to do with the original.

Usually the bill is not missing. It is in three places: the paper in a drawer, the PDF in email, the photo in the gallery. When you need the February gas bill, you search all three.

Which bills you have to archive and what form they come in

In an ordinary household in Romania, the list is roughly the same every month:

  • electricity, from your supplier (PPC, Electrica, Hidroelectrica, E.ON or another);
  • gas;
  • water and sewerage, if you have a direct contract with the operator;
  • apartment block maintenance fees, where you have the payment list from the notice board and the receipt from the owners' association;
  • internet, TV and phone.

Some come on paper, others only as a PDF by email or in the supplier's customer account. You scan the paper with the camera. For PDFs, the SageBills description on the App Store says you can also import a PDF.

SageBills has over 40 bill categories and suppliers preloaded for Romania. If your supplier is not on the list (for example an owners' association or a local water operator), you can add it as a custom supplier.

A photo the app can read

Text recognition works only with what it sees in the image. A few habits spare you corrections:

  • Good light, no shadows. Daylight is ideal. Do not stand between the lamp and the bill.
  • No glare. Glossy paper reflects the flash. Tilt the phone a little or turn the flash off.
  • The whole page, flat. Unfold the bill, put it on a flat, dark surface and get all four corners in the frame.
  • The page with the summary. On multi-page bills, what matters most is the page that shows the total, the due date and your customer details.
  • The phone still and parallel to the sheet. Blurry text is read wrong.

Scanning in SageBills, in short

The steps below are general. The exact button names may differ:

  1. You open SageBills and choose to add a new bill by scanning.
  2. You photograph the bill or import the PDF.
  3. The app extracts the data on the phone and shows it to you so you can check it.
  4. You correct what is needed, choose the category and save.

Everything happens on the iPhone. The SageBills page says that the image and the extracted text do not leave the device. There is no SageBills server and no account to create.

Check what the app read

Automatic recognition is fast, but not infallible, especially on crumpled paper. Before you save, check four things:

  • The amount. Many bills show several amounts: the month's total, the previous balance, the amount due. Check whether the app took the amount you actually pay. Watch the comma and the dot as well.
  • The date. A bill usually has an issue date, a billing period and a due date. Check whether the date that was read is the one you want.
  • The supplier. The bill often also shows the distribution operator, not only the supplier. For electricity they are two different companies, explained in the electricity bill guide.
  • The account number or customer code. If the app read it, compare it digit by digit with the bill. A wrong digit here is hard to notice later.

The same rule for every bill

An archive is useful only if it is consistent. Decide once and apply it the same way:

  • One supplier, one name only. Do not create "E.ON" one month and "Eon gaz" the next. If the supplier changes its name, note the change.
  • One category by the type of bill. Gas under gas, internet under internet. If you have an internet + TV + mobile package on a single bill, choose one category and stick to it.
  • Short notes. SageBills lets you add notes to bills. Use them for what does not fit elsewhere: "reconciliation after a meter reading", "paid from the joint account", "the original is in the 2026 bills folder".
  • The meter reading, if you have it. The app also keeps consumption readings (kWh, cubic metres). They are worth entering when they are on the bill.

Consistency also helps when you search. On iPhone, SageBills bills can be found through Spotlight, and a supplier name written the same way every time is easier to find.

What you do with the original

An important detail: SageBills keeps the bill's data, not necessarily the photo. The privacy policy says that the original image is not kept after scanning unless you choose otherwise.

So keep the original where you already have it, when you need the document itself:

  • PDFs stay in email or in a "Bills" folder in the Files app;
  • papers you need stay in a folder, by year;
  • in the bill's notes you write where the original is.

You need the document especially for a complaint to the supplier or a disagreement with the owners' association. For maintenance fees, the guide to the payment list explains which documents you can ask the association for.

If you have to show the expenses to someone, for example the owner when you rent, SageBills exports the records to CSV.

How you start

Do not move the whole drawer in one evening. Start with this month's bills, then scan them as they arrive. If you want, also set reminders for due dates.

SageBills is free, does not require an account and runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac. The details are on the SageBills page.
