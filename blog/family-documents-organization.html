How to organize your family's important documents

The simplest way to organize a family's documents is one folder per person plus one shared "household" folder, a consistent file-naming pattern, and a single calendar of every expiry date in the family. Children's documents need the most attention: their passports expire faster than adults', and each child needs their own health card. Add a plan for who can reach the papers if you can't, and you've covered most of what goes wrong. Here's how to set it up.

What to collect, person by person

Start with an inventory. For each family member, gather:

  • Identity: birth certificate, passport, ID card (from the age it's issued), residence permits.
  • Health: national health insurance card, European Health Insurance Card (EHIC), vaccination record, important medical letters, allergy and medication notes.
  • Insurance: health, travel, life and any policy that names them.
  • School: enrolment confirmations, report cards you need to keep, certificates.

And for the household: property or rental contract, home and car insurance, vehicle registration, utility contracts, and any documents about custody or guardianship.

Children's documents expire faster

Children's identity documents usually have shorter validity periods than adults', so they're the ones that catch families out before a trip. In Romania, for example:

  • Passports: a child's simple electronic passport is valid for 3 years under the age of 12, and 5 years from 12, according to Romanian press coverage of the rules (BZI, April 2026). A passport can't be extended; you apply for a new one.
  • ID cards: the first identity card is issued at 14, and for ages 14 to 18 it's valid for 4 years under the current rules (Capital.ro). Newer legislation allows an optional card for younger children at the parents' request; check the current rules with your local population records office (SPCLEP).

Other EU countries set their own periods, so look them up on your national government's site and write them into your expiry calendar.

Travel papers for children

A birth certificate isn't a travel document. To leave Romania, a Romanian child needs a valid passport, or an ID card from age 14. If a child travels with only one parent, the other parent's consent is required as a notarized declaration; if they travel with someone other than a parent, both parents' consent is needed (Euronews România, citing the Border Police and Law 248/2005). Keep scans of these declarations with the child's documents, and check the current requirements with the Border Police before you travel, since the rules have exceptions.

Health cards: one per person

The European Commission is explicit that each member of a family should have their own European Health Insurance Card, and that the card's validity period varies from one country to another. Your national health insurance provider issues it. Put every family member's EHIC expiry date in the calendar; a child's card expiring the week before a holiday is a common surprise.

Vaccination records are worth scanning too. They're often asked for by schools, doctors and when moving country, and paper booklets get lost.

A folder structure that works

Whether you use a vault app, a drive or physical binders, the same structure helps:

Family/
                  Household/   contracts, home & car insurance, vehicle papers
                  Ana/         ID, passport, health, school
                  Matei/       ID, passport, health, school
                  Mum/         ...
                  Dad/         ...

Inside each folder, name files so they sort sensibly and tell you what you need without opening them:

person – document – number (last 4) – expires YYYY-MM-DD

For example, Ana – Passport – 4821 – expires 2028-03-14. The expiry date in the name means a quick glance at the folder shows what's due.

Build one family expiry calendar

List every document with an expiry date: passports, ID cards, EHICs, insurance policies, vehicle registration and inspection, residence permits. Then set at least two reminders for each one: an early one with enough time to book an appointment and gather papers, and a final one a few weeks before. For the timing, see how to get reminded before your passport or ID card expires, including the validity rules many countries apply at the border.

You can do this in a shared family calendar, or in an app that keeps the date next to the document. SageDocs, which we make, tracks expiry dates with each document and reminds you on your device; it isn't a shared family vault, so one parent keeps the master copy.

Digitising the paper

A scan is only useful if you can read it when you need it. A few rules make the difference:

  • Scan both sides of ID cards, health cards and residence permits; the back often carries the details you'll be asked for.
  • Use good light and a flat surface, and check that every number is legible before you file the original away.
  • Name the file straight away using the pattern above, so it never sits unnamed in a downloads folder.
  • Delete the photo from your camera roll once it's in its proper place, and empty Recently Deleted.
  • Keep the originals. A scan helps you report a loss or fill in a form; it rarely replaces the original document at a border or an office.

Review once a year

Pick a fixed moment, such as the start of the school year or before the summer holidays, and spend half an hour on the family folder: check every expiry date against the calendar, replace scans of renewed documents, remove papers you no longer need, and confirm that the person who would step in for you still knows where everything is.

Who holds what

  • Originals: keep them together at home in one known place, ideally a fire-resistant box. Take originals only when you need them.
  • Digital copies: encrypted, not in the photo library or email. Our guide to storing passport and ID scans safely covers why.
  • Teenagers: once children carry their own ID card, consider giving them a copy of their own documents on their phone, stored safely.
  • Grandparents or carers: if they look after the children, give them what they'd need in a medical emergency (health card details, allergies), not full identity copies.

Plan for emergency access

The more securely your documents are encrypted, the more important this is. With end-to-end encryption, nobody can reset your password and open the files for your partner.

  • Apple Legacy Contact: Apple lets you name a Legacy Contact who can request access to data in your Apple Account after your death, which can include photos, notes, files and device backups. They need the access key you generate plus a death certificate. iCloud Keychain data such as passwords isn't included.
  • Encrypted vault apps: a Legacy Contact receives your iCloud data, but files encrypted by an app with its own key (as SageDocs does) remain unreadable without that key. If you use one, write down how to unlock it and keep that note with your will or with someone you trust.
  • A written index: a one-page list of what exists and where it is (without passwords) helps anyone who has to step in.

FAQ

Does every child need their own European Health Insurance Card?

Yes. The European Commission says each member of a family should have their own card, and validity periods vary by country.

Can a child travel abroad from Romania with a birth certificate?

No. A birth certificate isn't a travel document; a child needs a valid passport, or an ID card from age 14, plus a notarized parental declaration in some situations.

How long is a Romanian child's passport valid?

According to Romanian press coverage of the rules, a child's simple electronic passport is valid for 3 years under the age of 12 and 5 years from 12. Check the current rules with the passport service before applying.
