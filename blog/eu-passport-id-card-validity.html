EU passport and ID card validity, by country

In most EU countries an adult passport is valid for 10 years and an identity card for 10 years too, but the exceptions catch people out: Sweden and Portugal issue 5-year adult passports, Hungary’s adult ID card lasts 6 years, and Germany and Spain shorten validity for younger adults. Children’s documents usually last 2 to 5 years. This guide compares 11 countries, with each figure checked against an official government source.

What EU law says about identity cards

National ID cards are issued under national law, but the EU sets common rules for their security and validity. Council Regulation (EU) 2025/1208 requires identity cards to be valid for at least 5 and at most 10 years. Countries may issue cards valid for less than 5 years to minors, and for more than 10 years to people aged 70 and over (EUR-Lex).

The same regulation phases out older cards:

  • Cards without a machine-readable zone (MRZ), or that fail the minimum ICAO security standards, ceased to be valid at their expiry or on 3 August 2026, whichever came first.
  • Other cards that do not meet the new requirements stay valid until they expire or 3 August 2031 at the latest.
  • Cards held by people who were 70 or over on 2 August 2021 keep their validity until expiry if they meet the ICAO standards and have a functional MRZ.

That is why Italy’s paper ID card can no longer be used to travel, and why Czechia’s oldest “unlimited” cards lapsed in August 2026.

Travelling inside the EU: what counts as a valid document

EU citizens can travel within the EU and the Schengen area with either a valid passport or a national ID card. The document has to be valid on the day you travel, and driving licences, bank or tax cards are not accepted as travel documents. For trips outside the EU you need a valid passport and, for some countries, a visa; check the destination’s entry rules, including how long your passport must remain valid (Your Europe).

Patterns worth knowing

  • Shortest adult passports: Sweden and Portugal (5 years). If you hold one of these, you renew twice as often as a French or Dutch traveller.
  • Age thresholds differ a lot: full validity starts at 12 in Poland and Sweden, 18 in Romania, the Netherlands and Hungary, 24 in Germany, 25 for Portugal’s Citizen Card and 30 for Spanish passports.
  • Long-lasting cards for older people: 35 years in Czechia, no expiry from 70 in Romania, and more than 10 years in Spain.
  • Free or cheap ID cards: France and Poland charge nothing; Hungary renews an expired card free; Portugal charges €15–18.
  • Recent changes: German fees rose in February 2026, Romania moved to a single 265 lei passport fee in August 2025, and Italian paper ID cards stopped working for travel in August 2026.

How far ahead should you renew?

Where governments publish a processing time, it ranges from about 5 working days in the Netherlands and Portugal to about a month for a Polish passport. Some countries also limit how early you can apply; Italy accepts a new CIE from 180 days before expiry. Add time for booking an appointment, which in busy periods can take longer than production itself.

A practical rule: check every passport and ID card in your household once a year, and start the renewal at least three months before a document expires, or before a trip to a country that requires extra validity.

FAQ

How long is an ID card valid in the EU?

Under EU rules a national identity card is valid for at least 5 and at most 10 years. Countries may issue shorter cards to minors and longer ones to people aged 70 and over: Czechia issues 35-year cards to over-70s and Romania’s card has no expiry from 70.

Can I travel within the EU with an expired ID card or passport?

No. Your passport or national ID card must be valid on the day of travel. Driving licences, bank or tax cards are not travel documents.

Which EU countries issue 5-year adult passports?

Among the countries in this guide, Sweden and Portugal issue adult passports valid for 5 years. Spain issues 5-year passports to people under 30 and Germany 6-year passports to people under 24.

Are paper ID cards still valid?

Identity cards without a machine-readable zone stopped being valid on 3 August 2026 under the EU rules, unless they expired earlier. Other cards that do not meet the EU security standards remain valid until they expire or 3 August 2031 at the latest.
