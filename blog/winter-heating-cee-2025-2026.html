Winter Heating Costs in CEE: the 2026–2027 Season

The heating season now underway runs from 1 November 2026 to 31 March 2027. Romania is billing district heat with 11% cold-season VAT and a published Bucharest population price. Poland’s main household gas tariff has been extended to the end of 2026 at the same unit price. Hungary’s regulated household scheme is still the one on the bill. Czechia’s 2026 regulated charges still cover the rest of this calendar year, and the 2027 decision is not final. Figures below are only the ones we can tie to a source.

This guide covers the current season, winter 2026–2027. The page address still says 2025-2026 so existing links keep working. In Romanian: Încălzirea în iarna 2026–2027.

Updated 6 November 2026. Every price on this page links to a source at the bottom. Where a 2027 tariff is not yet a published decision, the page says so and does not fill the gap.

Romania: CMTEB price, 11% heat VAT, heating aid

District heating in Bucharest. Compania Municipală Termoenergetica București (CMTEB) lists a population price of 283.75 lei/MWh without VAT. For the cold season (November–March) the same page says household heat, and heat for hospitals, schools and other categories in OUG 130/2021, is taxed at 11% under Law 141/2025. Outside the heating season the rate is 21%, from 1 August 2025. With 11% VAT, 283.75 lei/MWh is about 315 lei/MWh (283.75 × 1.11 ≈ 314.96). CMTEB does not publish an official gigacalorie tariff. At the usual conversion of 1 Gcal = 1.163 MWh, the same price is about 330 lei/Gcal before VAT and about 366 lei/Gcal with 11% VAT (283.75 × 1.163 × 1.11 ≈ 366.3).

Checked on 6 November 2026, the tariff page places that population row under ADITBI general-meeting decision 35 of 3 September 2026 and still cites H.C.G.M.B. 13 of 31 January 2023, the decision that bills household heat in MWh. The page also lists network and quarter-plant tariffs, some of them applying until 31 December 2026. Those rows are not the population price. In August 2026 the press discussed a possible increase, citing Termoenergetica’s debts. The population price on the operator’s page is still 283.75 lei/MWh without VAT. Prices in other Romanian cities are set locally; this page does not invent them.

Gas. Households had a final price capped at 0.31 lei/kWh under OUG 6/2025 until 31 March 2026. From 1 April 2026, OUG 12/2026 runs until 31 March 2027, so it covers this whole winter. Each supplier calculates the bill from several components, and producers sell gas for household use at a regulated 110 lei/MWh. The energy minister said household bills would not exceed 0.31 lei/kWh. There is no newer national ANRE household tariff to quote here: compare the lei per kWh on your own invoice with offers in the ANRE comparator.

Heating aid (ajutor de încălzire). Law 226/2021 covers vulnerable households for the cold season 1 November 2026 – 31 March 2027. Applications filed by 20 November 2026 inclusive can include November. After that date, aid usually starts the following month. Digi24 (5 November 2026) and Capital (6 November 2026) both describe article 17 (7): a file submitted on 20 November can pay for all five months through 31 March 2027; the same file submitted later, for example on 23 November, usually starts in December. The law’s ordinary deadline is 15 October, and applications are still accepted during the cold season. Those reports also give income ceilings of 1,386 lei average net monthly income per family member and 2,053 lei net monthly income for a single person, plus an asset test. File the request at the town hall, or the sector hall in Bucharest. The mayor’s order sets the amount.

Electricity, for context. Eurostat reports that Romanian household electricity prices rose 58.6% in national currency between the second half of 2024 and the second half of 2025, the largest increase in the EU. Romania’s household electricity price cap had ended on 30 June 2025. That is a 2025 comparison, not a heat tariff for this winter.

Poland: household gas tariff held through December 2026

Household gas from the former PGNiG Obrót Detaliczny (now myORLEN) is sold under a tariff approved by the energy regulator, URE. From 1 July 2025 the tariff gas price fell by 14.8%. On 10 February 2026 URE approved a further cut to 197.29 zł/MWh. On 8 June 2026 the URE president extended that myORLEN tariff through 31 December 2026, kept the household gas price at 197.29 zł/MWh for groups W-1 to W-4 and the matching Z and S groups, and left the subscription fees unchanged. Distribution charges are still set separately by the network operator. That URE notice does not set a price for 1 January–31 March 2027, so this page does not quote one.

District-heating prices in Poland are also set through tariffs approved by the URE president, case by case for each licensed heat company, usually for 12 months at a time.

Hungary: regulated prices still the household scheme

Under the rezsicsökkentés scheme, households pay a regulated price for gas and electricity up to an annual average consumption level, and a much higher, still government-set, price above it. Eurostat lists Hungary with the lowest household electricity price in the EU in the second half of 2025: €10.82 per 100 kWh, against an EU average of €28.96. That Eurostat release is a 2025 comparison, not a new 2026–2027 tariff.

The scheme’s cost is still debated. An October 2026 opinion piece in Telex, citing a Nép­szava compilation, puts budget support for regulated energy prices at over 5,000 billion forints between 2022 and 2025. On 2 November 2026, economy and energy minister István Kapitány told Kossuth radio that a more targeted redesign was not prepared for next year’s budget and is not the ministry’s current focus, Portfolio reported. This page does not treat that debate as a change to the price on the bill.

Czechia: 2026 regulated charges still apply; 2027 is not final

In Czechia the Energy Regulatory Office (ERÚ) sets the regulated components of the bill, such as distribution, while suppliers set the price of the energy itself. For 2026 ERÚ announced an average 1.1% rise in the regulated electricity component for low-voltage customers and a 4.7% rise for households’ gas, citing the need for network investment and, for gas, consumption at a thirty-year low. Those 2026 charges still cover November and December 2026. The supplier part of the price is where households can still compare and switch.

As of 6 November 2026, this page does not cite a final ERÚ price decision for household regulated charges from 1 January 2027, so it does not quote a 2027 percentage for January–March.

What to check during winter 2026–2027

  • Romania: 11% VAT on household heat through March, the CMTEB population price of 283.75 lei/MWh without VAT if you are in Bucharest, your own city’s heat price if you are not, your gas price per kWh under OUG 12/2026, and a heating-aid file by 20 November 2026 if you want November covered.
  • Poland: the myORLEN tariff at 197.29 zł/MWh through 31 December 2026, then the next URE decision, plus your heat company’s own tariff.
  • Hungary: whether your consumption stays inside the average band. No reform of the scheme is cited here as adopted.
  • Czechia: the 2026 regulated charges for the rest of 2026, your supplier’s energy price, and the final ERÚ decision before treating any 2027 figure as the bill.
  • Everywhere: log your heating bill every month. Only then can you tell whether a rise came from the price or from your own consumption.

Questions

What VAT applies to household district heat in Romania this winter?

In the cold season, November to March, Bucharest’s heat supplier CMTEB applies 11% VAT to household heat under Law 141/2025. Outside the heating season the rate is 21%, from 1 August 2025. Winter 2026–2027 runs from 1 November 2026 to 31 March 2027.

What is the Bucharest population price for district heat?

CMTEB’s tariff page lists a population price of 283.75 lei per MWh without VAT. With 11% VAT that is about 315 lei/MWh. At 1 Gcal = 1.163 MWh, the same price is about 366 lei per gigacalorie including 11% VAT. CMTEB publishes the price in MWh, not as an official gigacalorie tariff.

Is Romanian household gas still price-capped?

The final cap of 0.31 lei/kWh under OUG 6/2025 ended on 31 March 2026. From 1 April 2026, OUG 12/2026 applies until 31 March 2027, so it covers all of winter 2026–2027. Producers sell gas for household use at a regulated 110 lei/MWh, and the energy minister said household bills would not exceed 0.31 lei/kWh. The bill depends on the supplier’s components; this page does not cite a new national ANRE household tariff.

When do Romanian heating-aid applications still cover November?

Under Law 226/2021 the cold season is 1 November 2026 to 31 March 2027. Applications filed by 20 November 2026 inclusive can be paid from November. After that date, aid usually starts the following month. Digi24 and Capital reported the rule on 5–6 November 2026, citing article 17 (7).
