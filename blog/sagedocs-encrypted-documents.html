SageDocs: what an encrypted document vault is for

In short: SageDocs keeps scans and sensitive documents in an encrypted vault, with access through a master PIN, Face ID or Touch ID. It ties documents to their expiry dates and sends local reminders. If you turn on iCloud sync, attachments are encrypted before they leave the device, according to the product documentation. The use is concrete: you find the policy when you need it and you track when it expires, without using the photo library as an archive of papers.

What SageDocs actually does

SageDocs is an app for iPhone, iPad and Mac, for identity documents, contracts, insurance and medical documents. The published requirements are iOS 17, iPadOS 17 and macOS 14 or later.

You can scan documents with the camera and keep attachments such as photos and PDFs. For organisation, the app handles the title, the issuer, the category, notes and the document dates. Attachments are encrypted with AES-256-GCM before storage, according to the privacy policy.

You also get expiry tracking, sharing of the document you choose, and export of the data in an encrypted package. iCloud sync is optional and lets you use the documents on compatible Apple devices. It should not be confused with a separate backup service or a recovery guarantee.

When it is useful in practice

Think of an insurance policy. You keep it in the vault, give it a recognisable title and check the expiry date. When you need a copy, you open the right document. When the date approaches, the reminder helps you not leave it forgotten in a folder.

You can organise a rental contract or medical documents you want close at hand in the same way. For documents with a deadline, enter the date from the document and check the notification permission. Do not assume that a scan automatically means a correct expiry date.

A reasonable start is to add only the documents you need often. Check that the scans are readable and review old copies in the photo library or in email. The guide on keeping ID and passport scans discusses these habits. For deadlines, see also the guide to document expiry.

Why encryption matters if the phone already has a passcode

Device protection and encryption inside the app have different roles. On a Mac, FileVault protects access to the data on the startup disk through the login password. On models with Apple silicon or a T2 chip, storage is already encrypted automatically, and FileVault adds the protection tied to authentication.

That does not turn a PDF sent from a folder into a file protected by a vault PIN. SageDocs adds encryption of the attachments and its own access control. The protections complement each other; they do not replace each other.

Encryption does not remove every risk either. If you open the document on a compromised device or send it to the wrong person, the vault cannot undo the consequences.

Local folder, cloud or encrypted vault?

A local folder without its own encryption is enough for documents that are not sensitive, and it does not require a dedicated app. It can benefit from the protection of the disk and the user account. It does not, by itself, add a separate PIN or expiry tracking.

The cloud does not automatically mean unencrypted files. Google Drive encrypts files in transit and at rest. Apple explains the same protection for iCloud in the standard setup. The difference from full end-to-end encryption is who can decrypt the data.

Encryption in transit protects the transfer. Encryption at rest protects stored data, but it can use keys managed by the provider. With E2EE, the provider does not hold the keys needed to read the protected content. iCloud Drive can get E2EE through Advanced Data Protection. Google also offers client-side encryption for some Workspace accounts, turned on by an administrator.

For SageDocs, the product page states that attachments reach iCloud already encrypted and that Apple cannot decrypt them. That is the stated advantage for their content, not a promise that every piece of metadata is invisible. The broader comparison is in the guide to iCloud and Google Drive for identity documents.

What to know before you use it

When sharing, SageDocs temporarily decrypts the document you choose. The copy the recipient gets is no longer protected by your vault. Check the address and send only what is necessary.

The product FAQ warns that documents cannot be recovered if you forget the master PIN and that the developer cannot reset the keys. Do not rely on support to intervene. The page also mentions permanent deletion, with no recovery vault.

SageDocs is worth considering if you have sensitive documents on Apple devices and you want the document, protected access and the expiry date in the same place. If you already have a well-organised encrypted system, the benefit to weigh is document management, not the assumption that any other solution would be unsafe.
