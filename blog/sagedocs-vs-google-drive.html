SageDocs vs Google Drive for your personal documents

For identity documents like passports and ID cards, SageDocs is the more private choice: files are encrypted on your device and only you hold the key, while Google Drive encrypts your files with keys Google manages. Google Drive is the better choice if you need Android, Windows or web access, want to share and collaborate on documents, or want Google to be able to help you back into your account. Here's the comparison point by point. We make SageDocs, so weigh our view accordingly; every Google detail below links to Google's own documentation.

Encryption: who holds the key?

Google states that all files uploaded to Drive are encrypted in transit and at rest. That protects your files from someone tapping the network or stealing a disk from a data centre. It doesn't stop Google itself from decrypting them: for its default encryption at rest, Google's documentation says "We own and manage the keys".

Google does offer client-side encryption, where files are encrypted before they reach Google, but its help page lists the requirements: a Google Workspace account and an administrator who has enabled the feature. A personal Gmail account can't turn it on.

SageDocs encrypts every document on your iPhone, iPad or Mac with AES-256-GCM before it's stored. The key is derived from your master PIN using PBKDF2-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations and kept in the Keychain, behind Face ID, Touch ID or the PIN. If you turn on iCloud sync, Apple receives only ciphertext. We run no servers that hold your documents.

Why it matters for IDs: a passport scan is valuable to an attacker. With provider-held keys, anyone who gets into your Google account, or any process with access to Google's keys, can read it. With device-held keys, a stolen iCloud password alone isn't enough.

Account recovery: the other side of the coin

End-to-end encryption has a cost. If you forget your SageDocs master PIN, your documents can't be recovered. There's no reset link, because nobody else has the key.

Google, by contrast, has an account recovery process: you answer questions, verify with a recovery email or codes, and ideally do it from a device and location you normally use. If it works, your files are all still there. For some people, especially anyone who worries more about forgetting a password than about who can read their files, that's a real advantage.

Search and scanning

Google Drive can turn a PDF or photo into searchable text: you open the file with Google Docs and it extracts the text. Google's own guidance says the file should be 2 MB or smaller, with text at least 10 pixels high and the page the right way up. SageDocs doesn't list text recognition as a feature; it's organised around documents, categories and expiry dates rather than full-text search.

Expiry reminders

This is where the two tools are built for different jobs. Google Drive is a file store, so expiry dates for your passport, ID card, insurance or vehicle registration have to live in a separate calendar or reminders app. SageDocs tracks them next to the document and reminds you on your device before they run out. See how to get reminded before your passport or ID expires for how much notice you'll want.

Sharing

Drive wins on collaboration. You can share a file with specific people (up to 600 addresses) as viewer, commenter or editor, or switch on link sharing so anyone with the link can open it. Google notes that expiry dates on shared access are only available to eligible work or school accounts.

That flexibility is also the risk with identity documents: a folder shared with "anyone with the link" stays open until someone remembers to close it. In SageDocs, sharing is deliberate and one document at a time: you decrypt the file you choose, share it (for example by email), and the temporary decrypted copy is wiped afterwards. There's no shared family space.

Platforms and cost

Google Drive runs almost everywhere: apps for Android and iOS, Drive for desktop on Windows and macOS, and the web. Every Google account includes up to 15 GB of storage shared across Gmail, Drive and Photos; paid Google One plans add more and are priced per country (in Romania, 100 GB was RON 9.99/month on 13 October 2026).

SageDocs is Apple-only: iPhone (iOS 17+), iPad (iPadOS 17+) and Mac (macOS 14+). It costs $2.99/month or $24.99/year after a 7-day free trial.

If you keep documents in Google Drive, make it safer

If Drive is the right tool for you, a few habits close most of the gap:

  • Turn on two-step verification for your Google account and keep your recovery email and phone up to date, so the recovery process works for you and not for someone else.
  • Keep identity documents in their own folder and never switch it to "Anyone with the link". Access set on a folder applies to every file inside it, so one careless share exposes everything.
  • Share single files, not folders, when someone needs a copy, and remove access when they're done.
  • Watermark the copies you send ("Copy for [company], [purpose], [date]") so a leaked copy is less useful.
  • Review "Shared with me" and your shared files a couple of times a year.

When Google Drive is the better choice

  • You use Android, Windows or Chromebooks, or need documents in a web browser.
  • You regularly share and edit documents with others: a lease negotiation, family paperwork, a renovation folder.
  • You'd rather have a recovery path than sole custody of the key.
  • Your files are mostly ordinary documents, not identity papers.

When SageDocs is the better choice

  • You want only your own devices to be able to read your passport, ID and contracts.
  • You want expiry dates tracked with the documents and reminders that run on your device.
  • You use Apple devices and don't want another account with another company.

Plenty of people use both: Drive for everyday files and shared folders, an encrypted vault for the handful of documents that would hurt most if they leaked. For a wider comparison of encrypted options, see the best encrypted document vault apps for iPhone.

FAQ

Can Google read my files in Google Drive?

Drive files in a personal account are encrypted in transit and at rest with keys Google manages, so Google's systems can decrypt them. Client-side encryption, where Google can't, requires a Google Workspace account with the feature enabled by an administrator.

Is it safe to keep a passport scan in Google Drive?

It's protected against outsiders by encryption and your account security, but not end-to-end encrypted. Use a strong password and two-step verification, avoid link sharing for identity documents, or keep them in an end-to-end encrypted vault instead.

Can I use SageDocs on Android or Windows?

No. SageDocs runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac only.
