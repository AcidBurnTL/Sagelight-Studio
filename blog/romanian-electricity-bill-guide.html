Understanding Your Romanian Electricity Bill: A Complete Guide

A Romanian electricity invoice packs a dozen separate charges onto one or two pages, and most suppliers never explain them. This guide walks through a typical household invoice line by line, with the regulated values that apply in 2026, then covers what differs between the big suppliers, how to change your approved power, and how to switch supplier.

Updated 13 October 2026. All figures are excluding VAT unless marked otherwise, and each one links to its source at the bottom of the page. Regulated values change once or twice a year, so treat your own invoice as the final word.

Who is who: supplier vs. network operator

Two companies are involved in every bill, even though you only pay one of them.

  • Your supplier (furnizor) sells you the energy and sends the invoice. You can choose it. The large ones are PPC Energie (formerly Enel Energie, renamed after PPC bought Enel's Romanian business in October 2023), Electrica Furnizare, Hidroelectrica and E.ON Energie România, plus many smaller suppliers.
  • Your distribution operator (operator de distribuție) owns the cables and the meter in your area. You cannot choose it; it depends on where you live. It reads the meter and handles anything physical, such as a new connection or a change in approved power.

An annotated sample invoice

Every supplier lays the invoice out differently, but ANRE requires the same core information on all of them: the billing period, your client code and POD, the meter readings and how they were obtained, the price components marked as regulated or not, and the self-reading window. Line by line

1 Client code and invoice number

The cod client identifies you with the supplier. You need it for the supplier's app, for payments and for any complaint. Keep it together with the POD (next point) somewhere you can find quickly.

2 Consumption place, POD and billing period

The POD (punct de delimitare) is the unique code of your meter point. It stays the same when you change supplier, and it is the code a new supplier will ask for. The billing period tells you which days of consumption you are paying for.

3 Meter readings and reading type

The invoice shows the old and new meter index and how the new one was obtained: read by the distribution operator, sent by you (autocitire), or estimated. When it is estimated, the next real reading corrects the difference, which is where most "surprise" bills come from. In its universal-service offer, Electrica notes that the meter is read physically at most every three months; in between, you either send the index yourself or get billed on an estimate.

4 Active energy: the part you negotiate

Energie electrică activă is the price of the energy itself, set by your contract. It usually already includes the small transport injection tariff (TG, 3.63 lei/MWh in 2026). This is the only part of the bill that changes when you switch supplier.

The household price cap that ran under OUG 6/2025 ended on 30 June 2025; since 1 July 2025 consumption is billed at the price in your contract. If you never chose a supplier, you are probably on serviciul universal (universal service) with a supplier of last resort, which publishes its own price. For example, Electrica Furnizare's universal-service energy price for January–March 2026 was 0.74892 lei/kWh including TG, before network charges and taxes.

5 Network tariffs: transport, distribution, system services

These pay for the grid and are set by ANRE. They are the same whichever supplier you choose, but distribution depends on your area.

  • Transport, extraction component (TL): 36.45 lei/MWh (0.03645 lei/kWh) since 1 January 2026, paid to Transelectrica.
  • Distribution (low voltage): the biggest regulated item. In Electrica's January 2026 offer it ranged from 0.29408 lei/kWh (Rețele Electrice România) to 0.36353 lei/kWh (Distribuție Energie Oltenia).
  • System services: 14.70 lei/MWh from 1 January 2026, lowered to 12.72 lei/MWh (0.01272 lei/kWh) from 1 October 2026 by ANRE Order 60/2026.

6 Contributions: green certificates, cogeneration, CfD

Three small per-kWh contributions fund energy policy and appear on every invoice:

  • Certificate verzi (green certificates): supports renewable generation. Suppliers billed 72.54 lei/MWh (0.07254 lei/kWh) as of January 2026. ANRE expects the average impact on the final invoice to stay at 14.5 euro/MWh in 2027.
  • Contribuție pentru cogenerare de înaltă eficiență: 13.60 lei/MWh (0.0136 lei/kWh), supporting combined heat-and-power plants.
  • Contribuție pentru contracte pentru diferență (CfD): a very small line (0.144 lei/MWh in ENGIE's January 2026 table) that funds Romania's contracts-for-difference support scheme for low-carbon generation.

7 Excise and VAT

Electricity used at home pays the non-commercial excise rate of 7.68 lei/MWh in 2026 under the Fiscal Code (the commercial rate is half that, 3.84 lei/MWh). VAT on electricity is 21%: Law 141/2025 raised the standard rate from 19% to 21% from 1 August 2025, and VAT applies to the whole sum, network charges and contributions included.

8 Total, due date and the self-reading window

The first page shows the total, the due date and, by ANRE rules, the period in which you can send your own reading (at least five days) and how to send it. In Electrica's standard terms, invoices are due 15 days after issue and no penalty interest is charged if you pay within 30 days after the due date; check your own contract, because terms vary.

Where the money goes. In Electrica's January 2026 universal-service offer, 100 kWh cost between 143.07 and 151.47 lei including VAT depending on the region. The energy itself was 0.749 lei of the 1.09–1.16 lei per kWh charged before contributions and taxes; the rest was grid tariffs.

Estimated vs. actual readings

If you do not send a reading during the window and the operator does not read the meter, you are billed on an estimate, usually based on your consumption history or the consumption agreed in your contract. The next actual reading reconciles everything at once. Two habits avoid most nasty surprises:

  • Send your index every month inside the window printed on the invoice.
  • If your consumption changes a lot (a new heat pump, a baby, working from home), ask your supplier to update the consumption estimate in your contract.

If you have a smart meter that is read remotely, you usually do not need to send anything; the invoice will say the reading came from the operator.

Supplier by supplier

The regulated lines are identical everywhere. What differs is the app, the reading window and how the invoice is laid out.

PPC Energie (formerly Enel Energie)

Customers manage everything in the myPPC app (iOS, Android, Huawei AppGallery) or at myppc.ppcenergy.ro. The index is sent from the app's Index section, the website or by phone during the reading window printed on the invoice; the invoice also shows when the distributor reads the meter. PPC relaunched myPPC with a redesigned interface in July 2026; existing logins kept working. In Bucharest, Ilfov, Giurgiu, Dobrogea and Banat the network operator behind PPC customers is usually Rețele Electrice România, the former Enel distribution business.

Electrica Furnizare

The MyElectrica app and myelectrica.ro let you send the self-read index, pay and see consumption history. You register with an email address plus the client code and consumption place number from the invoice. Electrica says the index must be sent in the period shown on your invoice. Electrica Furnizare is also the supplier of last resort designated by ANRE for universal service.

Hidroelectrica

Hidroelectrica takes readings through its client portal, the iHidro app or a free phone line, and only during a fixed window: reported in 2026 as the 22nd to the 26th of each month. The obligation applies to customers without a remotely read smart meter. The window has occasionally been extended (for example during an app outage in August 2025), so check the company's announcements if you miss it.

E.ON Energie România

E.ON customers use E.ON Myline (app and web), section "Autocitire / Transmitere index". The window lasts about a week and is printed on the first page of the invoice. Without a reading, E.ON bills on the consumption convention in your contract, so keeping that convention realistic in Myline matters. E.ON's network sister company, Delgaz Grid, operates the grid in Moldova.

Changing your approved power (putere aprobată)

Many guides mention a "reserved capacity" fee. Standard Romanian household invoices do not charge one: the regulated components are all billed per kWh. A few competitive offers add a fixed component, which the offer must state in lei/month or lei/day. What every connection does have is an approved power (putere aprobată), written in your connection approval (aviz tehnic de racordare, ATR) or connection certificate. It is the maximum power your connection is sized and approved for.

You may need more when you add a heat pump, an induction hob or an EV charger. Changing it is handled by the distribution operator, not by your supplier:

  1. Find your current documents. The ATR or connection certificate and your POD code (on the invoice).
  2. Ask an authorised electrician what power you need and whether your internal installation supports it.
  3. Submit a request to your operator (Rețele Electrice, DEER, Distribuție Energie Oltenia or Delgaz Grid). Operators publish a form for updating the ATR for a household consumption place (for example Distribuție Energie Oltenia's "Cerere actualizare ATR – loc consum casnic"), asking for the new total power, the owner's written consent and the documents listed on the form.
  4. Wait for the operator's answer. If the new power fits within what your connection can carry, it is an update of the existing approval. If it needs grid work, you receive a new ATR and possibly a connection fee and works before the new power takes effect.
  5. Get the updated certificate and keep it with your property documents.

Lowering approved power is rarely worth doing for a household, because there is no monthly capacity charge to save on.

How to switch supplier

Switching is free for households, and supply is never interrupted during the change. ANRE's steps:

  1. Check your current contract for its end date. Fixed-term, fixed-price contracts can carry an early-termination fee; most household contracts do not.
  2. Compare offers in ANRE's comparator on posf.ro. Compare the full price per kWh, not just the energy component, and check how long the price is guaranteed.
  3. Sign with the new supplier. You need your POD and client code from a recent invoice, ID and proof of your right to use the home.
  4. Let the new supplier handle it. It must start the process; the technical switch runs through the POSF platform within 24 hours.
  5. Pay the final invoice from your old supplier.

How to spot an error

  • Compare with the same month last year. ANRE requires invoices based on actual readings to include that comparison graph.
  • Check the reading type. A spike right after several estimated months is usually a reconciliation, not a price rise.
  • Check the regulated unit values against the ones above. A different value can be legitimate (they change on 1 January and sometimes mid-year), but it should match the date of the billing period.
  • Check the price period. Offers guarantee the energy price for a fixed period; after it ends, the supplier must notify the new price in advance.

If something does not add up, complain to the supplier first, in writing. If the answer does not resolve it, you can take it to ANRE.

FAQ

How much do green certificates cost on a Romanian electricity bill in 2026?

As of January 2026, suppliers bill 72.54 lei/MWh, or about 0.073 lei per kWh before VAT. On 200 kWh that is roughly 14.5 lei before VAT.

Is there a monthly capacity or subscription fee on household electricity bills?

Not as a standard regulated charge: all regulated components are billed per kWh. Some competitive offers add a fixed monthly or daily component, which the offer must state explicitly.

What VAT applies to electricity in Romania?

21%, since 1 August 2025 (Law 141/2025). It is charged on the total, including network tariffs, contributions and excise.

Why did my bill jump after a few cheap months?

Usually because the previous months were estimated and the first real reading corrected them. Sending your own index every month prevents this.

How long does it take to switch electricity supplier?

The technical switch runs through ANRE's POSF platform within 24 hours once the new supplier starts it, and it is free. Check your current contract for any fixed-term clause first.
