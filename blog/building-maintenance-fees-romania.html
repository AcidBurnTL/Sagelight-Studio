The Hidden Cost of Building Maintenance Fees in Romania

If you live in a Romanian apartment block, you pay a monthly intretinere — building maintenance fee — that typically ranges from RON 150 to RON 600 depending on the block’s age, size, and energy infrastructure. Most residents pay it without question because the invoice rarely explains what the money covers. That opacity is a problem: building administrators have been documented overcharging for everything from common area electricity to lift maintenance contracts.

What Intretinere Actually Covers

A legitimate intretinere invoice includes: common area electricity (stairwells, exterior lighting, basement), common area heating (if the block has central heating), lift maintenance contract (ascensor), cold water common areas, building insurance (required by Law 260/2008), administrator fee (administratie), and repair fund contributions (fond de reparatii). In blocks connected to district heating, a heat allocation component is also included based on your apartment’s share of the building’s total heat consumption.

What Is Legally Mandatory vs. What Is Add-On

Romanian law (Law 196/2018 on residential buildings) specifies that the administrator must provide a monthly statement itemising every expense. The total must be derived from actual invoices, not estimates, with the exception of the repair fund contribution which is set by an owners’ association vote. If you are receiving a single-line invoice with just a total amount, that is not legally compliant, and you are entitled to request the full breakdown.

Common Overcharge Patterns

Common area electricity double-billing: In some blocks, individual apartments are metered for their own consumption, but the administrator also allocates common area electricity in a way that exceeds the actual common meter reading. Request the common meter reading and the total allocated across all apartments — they should match.

Inflated lift maintenance: Lift maintenance contracts in Romania typically cost RON 200–500/month for a standard residential lift. If your block has one lift and the invoice allocates RON 800+, request the contract and verify.

Repair fund without a vote: Contributions to the repair fund must be approved by an owners’ association meeting. If you are paying into a repair fund but have never been invited to an association meeting, that is a procedural violation.

How to Audit Your Invoice

Request a full breakdown from your administrator in writing (an SMS or email is legally sufficient as documentary evidence). Cross-reference the common area electricity allocation against the meter reading from the distributor. Compare the administrator fee to the rate approved at the last association meeting. Log your intretinere in SageBills every month and flag months where the total increases without prior notification — unannounced increases are a common early indicator of irregularities.

The administrative effort to audit one year of intretinere statements is roughly three to four hours. For a household in a Bucharest block where overcharging is present, the typical recovery is RON 600–1,200 in credits or adjustments — approximately €120–€240.
