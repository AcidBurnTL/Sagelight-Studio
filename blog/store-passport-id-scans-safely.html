How to store passport and ID scans safely on your phone

Sooner or later you need a copy of your passport or ID card: for a rental application, a bank or phone contract, a hotel abroad, or simply as a backup if the original gets lost. Most people snap a photo and forget about it. That photo then lives in places you never chose: your photo library, its cloud sync, an email thread, a chat with a landlord. This guide covers where a scan should live, where it shouldn't, and how to hand over a copy without giving away more than you need to.

Why keep a digital copy at all?

A scan is genuinely useful. It lets you prove your identity when the original is in a drawer at home, gives you the document number and expiry date at a glance, and makes it much easier to report a lost or stolen document because you have every detail in front of you. The goal isn't to avoid copies; it's to keep them somewhere that only you can open.

What to scan, and what not to store next to it

  • Passport: the photo/data page. Other pages rarely matter unless you need a visa or residence stamp.
  • ID card: both sides, since the back often carries the address and document details.
  • Residence permits and driving licences: both sides, for the same reason.

Don't store the things that would turn a copy into a complete identity kit in the same place: PINs, passwords, answers to security questions, or photos of your bank cards. A thief who finds a passport scan is a problem; one who finds a passport scan next to your banking details is a much bigger one.

Is the camera roll safe for passport photos?

It's the most common place and one of the weakest. Three reasons:

  • It syncs. If iCloud Photos is on, the image is uploaded to iCloud. Under Apple's default "Standard Data Protection", Photos is one of the categories where the encryption keys are held by Apple, so Apple can decrypt it on your behalf, for example during account recovery (Apple).
  • Other apps can see it. Any app you've granted full photo-library access can read every image, including that passport page.
  • It's where attackers look. If someone takes over your Apple Account, scrolling the photo library for ID documents is the obvious first move, and those images can help them reset other accounts.

The Hidden album can be locked with Face ID, but it's still part of the same library and syncs with iCloud Photos like any other photo.

Why email and messaging apps are a poor archive

Email keeps attachments on the provider's servers indefinitely, searchable by anyone who gets into the account. Apple, for example, notes that iCloud Mail is not end-to-end encrypted even with Advanced Data Protection turned on, because it has to interoperate with the global email system (Apple).

Messaging apps may encrypt the conversation, but the copy then sits on the other person's phone and in both chat backups. WhatsApp only end-to-end encrypts backups stored in iCloud or Google Drive if you switch that option on yourself (WhatsApp). Once you've sent a scan, assume you no longer control it.

What "encrypted" should mean for identity documents

Almost every cloud service says your files are encrypted. The question that matters is who holds the key.

  • Encrypted in transit and at rest: the provider encrypts your data on the way to and on its servers, but keeps the keys. Google, for instance, states for its default encryption at rest: "We own and manage the keys" (Google Cloud).
  • End-to-end encrypted: only your devices hold the keys. The provider stores data it cannot read.

For a passport scan you want the second kind. On iCloud that means turning on Advanced Data Protection, or encrypting the file yourself before it ever reaches the cloud. We compare the options in detail in Is iCloud or Google Drive safe for identity documents?

A safer setup in five steps

  1. Use a strong device passcode and two-factor authentication on your Apple Account. Everything else depends on this.
  2. Keep scans in an encrypted vault, not the photo library. Scan straight into the vault, then delete the original photo and empty Recently Deleted.
  3. If you keep files in iCloud Drive, consider Advanced Data Protection. It extends end-to-end encryption to iCloud Drive, Photos, backups and more, but it's opt-in and requires a recovery contact or recovery key (Apple). It is not available to new users in the UK (Apple).
  4. Clean up old copies. Search your email for "passport" and "ID" attachments, and clear old chats where you sent scans.
  5. Record the expiry dates so the copy also works as a reminder. See how to get reminded before your passport or ID expires.

How to send a copy without oversharing

Before sending a scan, ask whether the recipient really needs a full copy; Dutch consumer organisation Consumentenbond recommends asking exactly that (Consumentenbond). The Dutch government goes further with its free KopieID app, which lets you cross out data the recipient doesn't need and adds a watermark with who the copy is for, the purpose and the date (Rijksoverheid). You can do the same with any markup tool: write "Copy for [company], [purpose], [date]" across the image. A watermarked copy is far less useful to anyone who intercepts it.

Rule of thumb: one encrypted master copy that only you can open, and a watermarked, single-purpose copy for each person who genuinely needs one.

FAQ

Is it safe to keep a photo of my passport on my phone?

Yes, if it's stored encrypted with a key only you control and protected by your passcode or Face ID. The risk comes from copies in the photo library, email and chats, which sync to servers and other devices.

Should I email my passport scan to myself as a backup?

It's better than nothing in an emergency, but it leaves a permanent, searchable copy on a mail server that isn't end-to-end encrypted. An encrypted vault with encrypted sync gives you the same backup without that exposure.

Does the Hidden album protect passport photos?

It hides them from casual scrolling and can be locked with Face ID, but the images remain part of your Photos library and sync with iCloud Photos like any other picture.
