Apple Notes locked notes vs the Files app vs a document vault: what's safe for your ID?

Short answer: a locked note in Apple Notes is genuinely end-to-end encrypted and is a reasonable free home for a photo of your passport, but it can't hold PDFs and it's easy to lose if you forget a separate notes password. The Files app is only as private as your iCloud settings: without Advanced Data Protection, Apple holds the keys to iCloud Drive. A dedicated document vault encrypts every file on your device and adds structure and expiry reminders, at the cost of another app (and, in our case, a subscription). The details below will help you pick the right one for your situation.

Full disclosure: we make SageDocs, a document vault. We've tried to describe Apple's built-in options as accurately and as fairly as Apple's own documentation allows, and we link to every source at the end.

Option 1: Locked notes in Apple Notes

Apple Notes lets you lock individual notes. Since iOS 16 you can choose between two ways to do it: your iPhone passcode (so there's nothing new to remember) or a separate password used only for Notes. For notes stored in iCloud, both options use end-to-end encryption, according to Apple. Using the device passcode requires iCloud Keychain to be turned on.

Under the hood, Apple's Platform Security guide says a 16-byte key is derived from your passphrase with PBKDF2 and SHA-256, and the note and its attachments are encrypted with AES-GCM before being stored in Core Data and CloudKit. The unencrypted original is then deleted.

Where locked notes fall short for documents

  • No PDFs. Apple lists notes that cannot be locked: notes with PDFs, audio, video, or Keynote, Pages or Numbers documents; notes with tags; shared notes; and notes in accounts synced over IMAP such as Gmail or Yahoo. The attachment types that can be secured are images, sketches, tables, maps and websites. If your bank or embassy sent you a PDF, it can't live in a locked note.
  • A forgotten Notes password is final. If you chose a separate Notes password and forget it, Apple says there is no way to access those locked notes. Resetting the password only protects notes you lock afterwards.
  • No structure. Notes is a notebook, not a records system. There's no field for an expiry date, no reminder before a passport runs out, and no way to group a car's paperwork together other than by hand.
  • It stays open for a while. After you unlock a note, Apple's guide says the secure session lasts until you tap Lock Now, the device locks, or Notes has been in the background for more than three minutes on iPhone (eight on Mac).

Good for: a free, encrypted place for a few photos of ID pages, if you're disciplined about the password.

Option 2: The Files app (iCloud Drive or "On My iPhone")

The Files app is a front end for two very different places:

  • On My iPhone keeps files in local storage. Apple's Data Protection gives every file its own 256-bit key, handled by the hardware encryption engine, so the data is encrypted at rest on the device. But nothing is backed up anywhere unless you back up the whole phone, and if the phone is lost, so are the files.
  • iCloud Drive syncs across your devices. Under Apple's default Standard Data Protection, iCloud Drive is encrypted in transit and on Apple's servers, but the keys are held by Apple, which means Apple can decrypt the data on your behalf (for example, to help you recover your account). Turning on Advanced Data Protection moves iCloud Drive, Notes, Photos and iCloud Backup to end-to-end encryption.

Advanced Data Protection is opt-in. It requires iOS 16.2 or later on every device signed in to your account, two-factor authentication, and a recovery contact or recovery key, because Apple can no longer help you recover the data. Apple has also stopped offering it to new users in the UK.

Since iOS 18 you can make any app, including Files, require Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode before it opens. That's useful against someone who picks up your unlocked phone, but it doesn't change how the files are encrypted in iCloud, and the lock applies only to that one device.

Good for: people who already use iCloud Drive and have turned on Advanced Data Protection, and who are happy to organise folders and remember renewal dates themselves.

Option 3: A dedicated document vault

A vault app encrypts each document itself, on the device, before anything is synced. That makes the protection independent of your iCloud settings: even with Standard Data Protection, the cloud only ever holds ciphertext.

What to check before trusting any vault:

  • Where encryption happens (on your device, not on the vendor's server) and which algorithm is used.
  • Who can recover your data. If the company can reset your access, it can also be compelled or breached into doing so.
  • Whether the company runs servers that see your files. Many vault apps sync through their own cloud accounts.
  • Which file types it accepts (PDFs and multi-page scans matter for real paperwork) and whether it tracks expiry dates.

How to choose in one minute

  1. Just a couple of ID photos? Use a locked note with your device passcode, and delete the original photos from your library (and Recently Deleted).
  2. You already live in iCloud Drive? Turn on Advanced Data Protection first, set up a recovery contact, then keep a dedicated folder for identity documents.
  3. A household's worth of passports, ID cards, insurance and car papers, with dates to track? That's what a vault is for.

Whatever you pick, the weakest link is usually the copy you forgot about: the original photo in your camera roll, an email attachment, or a chat. Our guide to storing passport and ID scans safely covers that clean-up.

FAQ

Are locked notes in Apple Notes encrypted?

Yes. Apple says locked notes use end-to-end encryption for iCloud accounts, whether you lock them with your device passcode or a separate Notes password. The note and its supported attachments are encrypted with AES-GCM.

Can I lock a note that contains a PDF of my passport?

No. Apple doesn't allow locking notes that contain PDFs, audio, video, or Keynote, Pages or Numbers documents. Supported attachments are images, sketches, tables, maps and websites.

Is the Files app encrypted?

Files stored on the iPhone are protected by the device's Data Protection encryption. Files in iCloud Drive are encrypted on Apple's servers, but Apple holds the keys unless you turn on Advanced Data Protection.

Does locking the Files app with Face ID make my documents end-to-end encrypted?

No. Requiring Face ID to open an app only controls access on that device. It doesn't change how iCloud Drive encrypts your files.
