Medical documents and insurance policies: how to keep them organized and encrypted for the family

In short: household medical documents and insurance policies deserve a specific place, not the photo library and not the email thread. In SageDocs you keep them in an encrypted vault on your devices, you tie them to the expiry date and you get a local reminder, for example before an insurance policy expires. When a copy is needed, you temporarily decrypt only the chosen document — the policy or a medical letter — and send it. “For the family” here means that you keep the household’s documents on your devices and send one document when it is needed, not that the vault is a shared space for the home.

In a home, these documents usually sit in a folder, in a photo on the phone and in a PDF that arrived by email. When you look for them, you are usually in a hurry. The problem is not only having them, but finding the document of the right person, knowing whether the policy is still valid, and being able to send a copy without emptying the whole folder.

SageDocs is an encrypted vault for identity documents, contracts, insurance and medical documents, on iPhone, iPad and Mac (iOS 17+, iPadOS 17+, macOS 14+). How the vault is built is on the product page and, in short, in the guide to encrypted documents. Below is only about using it for the medical documents and the policies in the home.

What “for the family” means, in practice

SageDocs is a personal vault, on your devices. If you turn on iCloud sync, only encrypted content reaches the cloud, and the documents move between your iPhone, your iPad and your Mac, through your account. It is not a common vault, opened automatically to the other people in the home.

In practice, you keep in your vault the documents you look after: the home policy, the car’s RCA, the health insurance, the lab results or the medical letters you keep for yourself or for a family member. When someone needs a document, you send only that document.

If you also keep copies of a child’s documents, they stay in your vault. According to the privacy policy, Sagelight apps are not intended for children under 16.

What is worth having at hand

It is not a mandatory list and it does not replace a doctor’s or a lawyer’s advice. It is a starting point, with the documents you usually look for in a hurry.

For the medical side, for example: the health-insurance card or policy; medical letters, discharge papers or results you know will be asked for again; a summary of the treatment or of the allergies, if you already have it from the doctor; vaccination booklets or certificates, where you use them.

For insurance, the same household usually has several policies, with different expiry dates:

  • car insurance (RCA and, if you have it, CASCO) — the deadlines for RCA and for the car’s other documents are in the guide to the car documents;
  • the compulsory home insurance (PAD) and, if you have it, the optional one — what you keep after a purchase is in the guide to the documents after buying a car or a home;
  • other policies you renew from time to time, for example health or travel.

A digital copy helps you find the policy number or send a document. It does not replace the original where the paper is required. SageDocs is a personal management tool, not a legal archive and not a substitute for official registers.

Why a vault is worth it, not the photo library

A medical letter or a policy contains data you do not want to leave in a photo in the library, in an old email or in a chat. In SageDocs, each attachment is encrypted on the device with AES-256-GCM, before it is stored. Access is by the master PIN, Face ID or Touch ID, and the keys stay in the Keychain. Not a single byte leaves for Sagelight, and there is no telemetry.

If you turn on iCloud sync, Apple receives only ciphertext. If you forget the master PIN and you cannot sign in biometrically, the documents cannot be recovered: there is no reset of the keys.

How the encryption is built is already in the guide to the encrypted document vault. Why the photo library and email are a weak place for copies of documents is explained in the guide to ID and passport scans; the same reasoning holds for a policy or a medical document.

How you tell them apart when there are several people

When the vault holds documents for more than one person, the title has to say whose document it is, not only what it is. “Health policy – Maria” and “Medical letter – Andrei – 2026” can be found. Two files named only “Lab results” cannot.

The category follows the type of document: medical documents go in the medical category, policies in insurance. The issuer is who issued the document — the clinic, the laboratory or the insurance company — written as on the paper. In the notes you can keep what helps you use it, for example where the original is, not sign-in details or PINs.

The full rule for these fields is in the guide to organizing documents. What matters here is consistency: the same kind of document, the same kind of title, with the person’s name when it is needed.

Policies expire. The reminder stays on the phone

A policy with no expiry date is a document you can find, but one you can forget. In SageDocs you can tie the document to the expiry date and you get a local reminder, on the device. The product page gives insurance itself as the example, alongside the passport, the ID and the vehicle registration.

Enter the date from the document, not an approximate date. A reminder does not renew the policy and does not replace checking with the insurer. For the ID card and the passport, the deadlines are in the guide to identity-document expiry.

When you have to send a single document

Often you do not need the whole folder, but a policy for a claim or a medical letter for an appointment. You can temporarily decrypt and share only the chosen document; the temporary file is securely deleted after sharing. The SageDocs page uses precisely the example of sending the insurance card.

The copy you send leaves the vault: the recipient receives it as an ordinary file. Check whom you send it to, and do not attach more than you are asked for. There is also an encrypted export, if you want to take the data with you; it is a separate function, and it is not the same thing as sending a single document.

How to start without moving the whole archive

First choose the documents you look for most often and the ones that expire: this year’s policies and two or three medical documents you have kept searching for. Fill in the title, the category, the issuer and the expiry date, check that the scan is readable, then add the rest.

If you want to see what the app does beyond this guide, start from the SageDocs page.

The guide is practical and is not medical or legal advice. A copy on the phone does not replace the original and does not tell you which documents you are obliged to keep.
