Lost your passport abroad? What to do as an EU citizen

Short answer: report the loss to the local police and get a written record, then contact your country's embassy or consulate, which can issue an emergency travel document to get you home. If you're outside the EU and your country has no embassy or consulate there, any other EU country's embassy can issue an EU Emergency Travel Document. If you're travelling inside the EU, a valid national ID card may be enough to keep going. A scan of your passport won't let you cross a border, but it makes every one of these steps faster.

Step 1: Make sure it's really lost, then report it to the police

Retrace your steps and ask at your hotel, transport operator or the venue where you last had it. If it doesn't turn up, go to the local police and declare it lost or stolen. Ask for a written certificate or report.

You'll need it: Romanian consulates, for example, list a certificate from the local police confirming you reported the theft among the documents for an emergency travel document. A report also protects you if someone later misuses your passport.

Step 2: Contact your embassy or consulate

Your own country's embassy or consulate is the first port of call. Most EU countries issue some form of emergency or temporary travel document for exactly this situation. Have the following ready:

  • the police report;
  • any other identity document you still have (an ID card, an expired passport, a driving licence);
  • passport photos, if the consulate asks for them;
  • your travel details, so the document covers your route home.

Example: Romanian citizens

Romanian missions issue a titlu de călătorie (travel title) to citizens who no longer have a valid travel document, so they can return to Romania. According to the consulates' own pages:

  • it is valid for at most 30 days and ends automatically when you enter Romania;
  • you must apply in person (photo and signature are captured), and that includes children, who must be accompanied by their parents;
  • you'll need a Romanian identity document with a photo (for example an ID card or an expired passport) and the local police certificate if the passport was stolen.

Fees and opening hours differ between missions, so check the page of the specific embassy or consulate before you go. Back in Romania, you apply for a new passport as usual.

Step 3: No embassy of your own? Use another EU country's

EU citizenship gives you a right that many travellers don't know about. If you're in a country outside the EU where your own country has no embassy or consulate (or it can't easily be reached), any other EU country represented there must help you, and can issue an EU Emergency Travel Document (EU ETD).

  • A new, more secure EU ETD format has been issued since 9 December 2025: a sticker on a passport-sized paper form.
  • It's for a single journey home (or, exceptionally, to another destination), and its validity normally doesn't exceed 15 calendar days.
  • The embassy checks your identity and nationality with your home country first. In normal cases the procedure shouldn't take more than seven working days.
  • Fees are the same as the issuing country charges its own citizens, so they vary.
  • You must hand the document back when you arrive.

Travelling inside the EU? Your ID card may be enough

EU citizens can travel within the EU with either a valid passport or a valid national ID card. If you still have your ID card, you may be able to continue your trip or get home without an emergency document. Check with your airline before you go to the airport. Two things don't work: a driving licence (the EU says licences, bank cards and tax cards are not travel documents and may be refused as proof of identity) and a photo of your passport.

If you have no document at all at a border, the EU says authorities must give you every reasonable opportunity to obtain the documents or to prove your EU citizenship by other means, but proving it is your responsibility.

Why a digital copy helps, and what it can't do

A scan or photo of your passport's data page is not a travel document. It won't get you onto a plane or across a border. What it does do:

  • gives the police and the consulate your passport number, issue date and authority immediately, instead of you guessing them;
  • helps a consulate confirm who you are, especially if your other ID was lost too;
  • keeps the expiry date in view, so a passport doesn't run out just before a trip.

The copy needs to be somewhere you can reach when your phone is the only thing you have left, and somewhere a thief can't. Common mistakes that cost travellers days

  • Booking the flight home before you have a document. The emergency document is issued for a specific journey, and the consulate may need several working days. Ask how long it will take first, then book.
  • Skipping the police report. Consulates often ask for it, and going back to a police station later can cost you a day.
  • Arriving without any proof of identity. If everything was stolen, bring whatever links you to your identity: a copy of the passport, a photo of your ID card, a document with your personal number. The more the consulate can verify quickly, the sooner you travel.
  • Forgetting the children's documents. Children need travel documents too, and consulates such as Romania's require minors to attend in person with their parents.
  • Not checking the route. An emergency document is usually valid for one journey home. Transit through a third country, or a stopover, can be a problem, so tell the consulate your full route.

Before your next trip: a 3-minute checklist

  1. Scan the data page of every passport and ID card in your household into an encrypted place.
  2. Save the address and phone number of your embassy at your destination, and know which other EU countries are represented there.
  3. Check expiry dates: some destinations require a passport valid for months beyond your stay. Our guide to passport and ID expiry reminders covers the common rules.
  4. Travel with a second ID document stored separately from your passport.

FAQ

Can I travel home with a photo of my passport?

No. A photo or scan is not a travel document. It helps the police and your consulate identify you and issue an emergency document faster.

What is the EU Emergency Travel Document?

A single-journey document that any EU country's embassy or consulate can issue to an EU citizen whose passport was lost or stolen outside the EU, where the citizen's own country isn't represented. Its validity normally doesn't exceed 15 calendar days.

How long is a Romanian titlu de călătorie valid?

At most 30 days, and it ends automatically when you enter Romania. You must apply in person at a Romanian embassy or consulate.

Can I fly within the EU without a passport?

EU citizens can travel within the EU with a valid national ID card instead of a passport. Check with your airline before you fly too.
