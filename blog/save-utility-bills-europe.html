How to Cut Your Utility Bills: A 7-Step Household Audit

Household energy got more expensive across much of Europe, and in some countries sharply: Eurostat reports that Romanian household electricity prices rose 58.6% between the second half of 2024 and the second half of 2025, the largest increase in the EU. The good news is that a few hours of structured checking catches most of what is avoidable. There is no magic number. How much you save depends on your contracts and your habits, but the method below works in any country.

The core idea: you cannot manage what you do not measure. Before switching anything, know exactly what you pay today, line by line.

Step 1: Build a complete bill inventory

List every recurring bill: electricity, gas or district heating, cold and hot water, building maintenance (întreținere in Romania, czynsz to the housing community in Poland), internet, TV, mobile, waste collection and any service contracts. For each one note the provider, the monthly amount, the billing period and the contract end date. Pull the last three months of invoices so you have a baseline.

Step 2: Look for anomalies before you switch

Compare each category with its own history. Jumps usually have a mundane cause: an estimated meter reading that is corrected months later, a price change at contract renewal you did not notice, or a billing error that repeats every month. In Romania, ANRE requires electricity invoices to show your consumption compared with the same period last year, which makes seasonal jumps easy to spot. Submitting your own meter reading before the billing cutoff avoids most estimate-driven surprises.

Step 3: Check your electricity contract against current offers

Answer three questions: is your price fixed or variable, when does the contract end, and what do you pay per kWh compared with what is on offer today? Use the regulator’s own comparison tool rather than ads: in Romania, ANRE’s offer comparator; in Poland, URE’s Masz Wybór service. In Romania switching is free for households, and the technical switch takes 24 hours through ANRE’s POSF platform. Watch for early-termination fees on fixed-price, fixed-term contracts.

Step 4: Treat gas and district heating separately

District heating is usually a local service you cannot switch, so focus on what you control: the accuracy of your heat-cost allocator or meter readings and how heat is split in your building. For gas, compare offers the same way as for electricity, but check how prices are set in your country first. Romania, Poland and Hungary all had regulated or capped household gas prices in recent years, and the rules changed in 2025–2026.

Step 5: Check water and building costs

In apartment buildings, shared costs are divided using a formula. Ask your administrator for it and check your share against your own readings. In Romania, Law 196/2018 gives every owner the right to see the invoices behind the monthly list and to contest it within 10 days of posting. Our guide to întreținere walks through it.

Step 6: Review internet, TV and mobile together

Telecoms are where loyalty costs most. Note when each contract ends: that is when you have leverage. Keeping the signed contracts in one place, with their end dates, means you never miss that window (our document-keeping guide covers what to file and for how long). Compare a bundle with separate contracts at the same speed and data allowance, and read the length of the commitment before signing; bundle prices are usually tied to a minimum contract term.

Step 7: Track every month

A one-off audit catches the obvious problems. Monthly tracking catches the next ones at the moment they happen, not six months later, and gives you the history you need to argue with a provider. Twenty minutes a month is enough: log each bill when it arrives and look at the categories that moved.

FAQ

Is switching electricity supplier free in Romania?

Yes. ANRE states that switching is free for households, and the technical switch takes 24 hours through the POSF platform. Fixed-price, fixed-term contracts may carry an early-termination fee.

Where can I compare energy offers officially?

In Romania, use ANRE’s offer comparator linked from its switching guide. In Poland, use URE’s Masz Wybór service at maszwybor.ure.gov.pl.

Why did my bill jump in one month?

The most common causes are an estimated meter reading being corrected, a price change at contract renewal, or a billing error. Compare with the same month last year and submit your own meter readings to avoid estimates.
