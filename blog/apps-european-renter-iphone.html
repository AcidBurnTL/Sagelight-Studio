5 Apps Every European Renter Should Have on Their iPhone

Renting in a European city means a surprising amount of admin: utility bills in a language you may not read well, a contract and inventory you will need again at move-out, payments in more than one currency, and repairs that do not always get done quickly. These five iPhone apps cover that workflow without overlapping. Two of them are ours, and we say so.

1. SageBills: track every utility bill

SageBills is our free utility tracker. It covers 40+ bill categories, reads invoices with on-device OCR, and keeps everything on your iPhone, iPad or Mac, with optional iCloud sync and no account. For renters, the useful part is seeing the autumn-to-winter heating jump coming and checking that what the landlord passes on matches the actual invoices.

2. SageDocs: keep the rental paperwork safe

Every renter collects a small archive of documents that matter at the worst possible moment: the rental contract, the move-in inventory and photos, the move-out report, insurance policies, and ID scans the landlord asked for. They should not live in your camera roll or a chat thread.

SageDocs, also ours, is an encrypted vault for exactly this: AES-256-GCM encryption on your device, Face ID / Touch ID plus a master PIN, optional encrypted iCloud sync, and expiry reminders, so you get a nudge before the lease term or an insurance policy runs out.

3. Wise or Revolut: pay across currencies

If you earn in one currency and pay rent or bills in another, a multi-currency account saves real money compared with a traditional bank transfer. Wise converts at the mid-market rate and shows its fee upfront. Revolut includes a monthly allowance of currency exchange that depends on your plan, with extra fees above it and, on some plans, at weekends. Check each one’s current fee page for the amounts you send.

4. Google Translate: read invoices in another language

Google Translate can translate text through your iPhone’s camera: point it at an invoice or a notice from the building and read it in your language. It is not a legal translation, but it is enough to tell a routine bill from one that needs action.

5. A local repairs marketplace

When a repair is your responsibility, or the landlord agrees you can book it yourself, a marketplace with reviews and written quotes saves time. In Poland, Fixly, an OLX Group service, lets you post a job and get offers from local professionals. Its reviews are mixed, so check each contractor’s history and agree the price and scope in writing. Elsewhere, look for the established local equivalent, and keep the quote and invoice in the same place as your contract.

Bonus: SageMeet. If you record calls or meetings with a letting agent or landlord, where local law allows it, SageMeet, our third app, transcribes them with Whisper and turns them into searchable notes using your own OpenAI key, so verbal agreements do not get lost. Private Mode sends nothing anywhere.
